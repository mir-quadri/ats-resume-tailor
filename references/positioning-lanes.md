# Positioning Lanes

Pick one lane. Hold it. Write the boundary you must not cross.

## What is a lane?

A positioning lane is the identity the resume leads with. It determines what goes in the summary, what goes in the top third, and which achievements get emphasis. The same person can be a "data engineer who leads small teams" or a "people leader who is technically grounded." Different resumes, same person.

**Example lanes:**
- "Individual contributor in backend systems"
- "Technical leader transitioning to product management"
- "Full-stack engineer specializing in user-facing performance"
- "Data analyst building real-time dashboards for operations"

## Choosing a lane

1. **Start with the JD.** What identity does the job description assume?
2. **Cross-check the strengths.** Where are your verified wins?
3. **Check the fit assessment.** Are you going conditional because you're a strong individual contributor applying for a lead role? That is a lane mismatch, not a strength.
4. **Ask yourself:** If I could only emphasize one story about myself in this resume, what is it?

## Lane discipline

Once you pick the lane:
- **Summary** argues for this identity.
- **Top third** (first role, first section) showcases the strongest evidence.
- **Everything else** is sorted by relevance to the lane, not chronology.
- **Skills** list emphasizes what the lane needs.
- **Anything that undercuts the lane** is de-emphasized or removed. If the lane is "data engineer" and the strongest achievement is "managed a remote team," you can include it, but it is not the headline.

## Seniority framing

A lane also implies seniority. "Senior engineer" and "engineer" are different lanes. "Manager" and "coordinator" are different.

Use the JD to guide. If the role is titled "Senior Software Engineer" and the JD emphasizes "team leadership," do not position yourself as an individual contributor. That is a lane mismatch.

If the role does not specify seniority, default to parity with the role title. If you are applying for "Software Engineer" and have senior experience, consider positioning as senior (you are stronger than the baseline) or as mid-level (you are more available and eager). Fit assessment calls this out.

## The boundary

Before you write, state it plainly:

> **Positioning lane: Data engineer specializing in pipeline architecture.**
>
> **Boundary: Avoid emphasizing manager experience. It is real but not the lane.**

This becomes a checklist for the final review. Every achievement that goes in must fit the lane. Anything that violates the boundary gets cut.
