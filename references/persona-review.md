# Multi-Persona Review

Run this before every delivery. Read the finished resume four times, once as each persona, and
write down concrete findings. Then fix and re-verify. The point is to catch, from each angle,
what that reviewer would reject.

## Persona 1: The ATS (keyword parser)

Reads the file mechanically. No judgment.

Check:
- Target title or an honest variant appears near the top.
- Standard section headings (SUMMARY, EXPERIENCE, SKILLS, EDUCATION, CERTIFICATIONS).
- Single column, no text boxes, no content trapped in headers/footers or images.
- JD must-have keywords are present where true, acronyms spelled out once.
- No em/en dashes (the validator confirms this).

Red flags: creative headings, multi-column layout, missing must-have terms, keywords only in a
graphic. Fix by adjusting structure and honest keyword placement.

## Persona 2: The recruiter / HR screener (6-second skim)

Skims for must-haves before deciding to forward the resume.

Check (all visible in the top third or an obvious section):
- Right title and seniority.
- Enough years of experience, stated plainly.
- Location and work model if the JD cares.
- Required certifications and degree, easy to find.
- No unexplained employment gaps.

Red flags: value proposition buried below the fold, years not obvious, certs hidden at the very
bottom when the JD requires them, a generic summary that could belong to anyone.

## Persona 3: The hiring manager (evidence check)

Asks: can this person actually do MY job, and did they own outcomes at this scope?

Check:
- Each of the JD's top three priorities has a matching, quantified, owned bullet in the top half.
- Bullets lead with outcomes, not activities.
- Scope numbers (headcount, budget, program count) signal the right level.
- Most recent and most relevant experience carries the most detail.
- The story is coherent: the summary, the "what I bring" bullets, and the experience agree.

Red flags: activity lists with no outcomes, scope that reads junior for the target level, the
strongest evidence hidden in an old role, claims that do not connect to a real project.

## Persona 4: The skeptical interviewer (defensibility check)

The most important for zero hallucination. Reads every line asking: "If I probe this for three
follow-up questions, does it hold?"

Check, bullet by bullet:
- Every metric traces to the fact ledger and to work the candidate owned.
- Every tool and skill is one the candidate can discuss in depth.
- No borrowed company framework is claimed as the candidate's own delivery.
- Ranges and numbers match the source (no quiet inflation).
- Nothing implies a title, team size, or result larger than the source supports.

Red flags: any claim the candidate would have to walk back, any number not in the source, any
buzzword with no lived experience behind it. Fix by cutting, softening, or re-scoping to the truth,
or by asking the candidate to confirm before keeping it.

## After the four passes

Compile the findings, apply the fixes, then rerun the validator and the visual render. Only ship
when all four personas pass and the programmatic checks are green.
