# Fit Assessment (Run Before Writing Anything)

The most valuable thing this skill does is sometimes tell the candidate not to apply. Job
searching at any level is a budget problem: finite hours, finite referrals, finite emotional
energy. A polished resume for a role the candidate will not get spends all three for nothing.

**Analogy.** A good tailor measures you before touching the fabric, and a good tailor will tell
you the suit does not suit you. Cutting first and discovering the mismatch later wastes the cloth.

## When to run it

Always, before opening a document. Even when the candidate says "just build it." The assessment
takes one message and they can override it in one word.

## How to build the assessment

**Step 1. Decompose the JD into requirement classes.**

- **Hard requirements.** Degree, years, certifications, clearance, license, location, work
  authorization. Binary. Either met or not.
- **Core responsibilities.** What the person actually does day to day. If the JD gives percentage
  weightings, use them. If not, infer weight from ordering and repetition.
- **Identity requirements.** Who the posting says the person IS: "a seasoned consultant", "a
  hands-on practitioner", "a builder", "a people leader". These are the sneakiest, because a
  candidate can match most of the responsibilities while being the wrong species. Weight them
  heavily.
- **Preferred qualifications.** Nice to have. Never disqualifying alone, but a stack of misses
  here signals the role is aimed elsewhere.

**Step 2. Score each against the fact ledger.** Evidenced, partial, or absent. Partial means the
candidate has done an adjacent version and can defend the adjacency under questioning.

**Step 3. Check the level and shape fit.** Separate from skills:

- **Seniority.** Compare the JD's minimum years and management bar to the candidate's. Applying
  far above the target band reads as overqualified and often gets auto-rejected. Applying far
  below reads as a stretch.
- **Compensation.** If a band is posted, compare it to the candidate's current level. A band well
  below their current comp is a real signal about the level, not just the pay.
- **Location and work model.** On-site, hybrid, travel percentage, time zone.
- **Sector shift.** Moving industries costs credibility that has to be paid for somewhere.

## How to deliver the read

Lead with the verdict. Then the evidence. Keep it short enough to read on a phone.

1. **Where they are genuinely strong.** Tie to the JD's own weightings so the candidate can see
   how much of the role they cover.
2. **Real gaps.** Name them without softening. Say which one is the biggest risk and why.
3. **A number and a recommendation.** A rough percentage fit plus one of three calls:
   - **Go.** Strong match. Build it.
   - **Conditional.** Real gaps exist but honest compensating material exists too. Say exactly what
     the candidate would be signing up for, then let them decide.
   - **Skip.** The gaps are in the parts of the role that cannot be reframed. Say so and explain
     what kind of role would be the better use of the same hours.

## What makes a gap uncoverable

Some gaps yield to honest reframing and some do not. The dividing line is whether the candidate
has done a genuinely adjacent version of the work.

**Coverable.** The candidate did the substance under a different label, at a different scale, or
for an internal rather than an external audience. Training colleagues against a posting that asks
for teaching experience. Running the same function at a smaller site. A required certification that
is genuinely in progress.

**Not coverable.** The candidate has never done the thing in any form. Managing people when they
have only coordinated projects. Owning a budget when they have only spent against one. Serving
paying clients when they have only served internal colleagues. Practicing a licensed profession
without the license. Reframing these is not positioning, it is lying with extra steps, and it
collapses in the first screening call.

When a role's core is built on an uncoverable gap, recommend skipping. When it is built on
coverable gaps, recommend conditionally and be explicit about what the interview will feel like.

## Stop and wait

End the assessment and stop. Do not assess and then build in the same turn. The candidate's
decision is an input to the build, not a formality afterward. If they say build it anyway, build
it, and do not relitigate the gaps in the delivery summary beyond noting what stayed open.
