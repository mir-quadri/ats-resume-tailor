# Fit Assessment

Assess the candidate's fit for the role before writing anything. Never skip this step.

## Process

1. **Extract the JD requirements.** List must-haves and nice-to-haves with any weighting language.
2. **Scan the source resume for evidence.** For each requirement, find the strongest true evidence.
3. **Build the three lists.**
   - **Strengths:** JD requirements where the candidate has clear, verified evidence.
   - **Gaps:** JD requirements with no honest match or only unverified claims.
   - **Concerns:** Gaps that are deal-breakers for this specific role.

4. **Never include unverified metrics as strengths.** If the only evidence for a requirement is an unverified claim (e.g., a metric the candidate didn't personally confirm), that requirement is unevidenced. It goes with the gaps, not the strengths, pending confirmation.

## Verdict format

Provide all three parts:

1. **Strengths.** Where the candidate is strong, tied to the JD's weightings. One to three sentences.
2. **Gaps.** Named plainly, with the biggest risk flagged. One to three sentences.
3. **Recommendation with percentage.**
   - Rough percentage alignment (e.g., "75%").
   - One of three calls: **go** (strong fit), **conditional** (worth applying with caveats), or **skip** (not worth your time).
   - Can be hedged ("conditional, leaning skip" is valid).

Example:

> **Strengths.** Five years of hands-on Python and data pipeline work. Direct experience with AWS and Redshift. Led a team of two data engineers for 18 months.
>
> **Gaps.** No Spark experience, no published ML models. The "build predictive models" part is gap territory right now.
>
> **Fit: 65%, Conditional.** You have the data engineering foundation they want, but the ML modeling expectation is real. If you're willing to ramp quickly on Spark and can point to math coursework or personal projects, it's conditional-go. If modeling is truly required day-one, it's conditional-skip.

## Non-negotiables

- Never invent evidence. A gap is a gap.
- Never promote an unverified claim to a strength, even temporarily.
- Always give a percentage and a clear call (go/conditional/skip).
- Base fit on the JD's stated requirements, not on what a similar role at your company asked for.
